🧬 Quick Genetics Overview

Every trait comes from a pair of genes — one from each parent. Some genes are dominant (they show whenever a cat carries even one copy) and some are recessive (they only show when a cat carries two copies).

Why some genes are unknown. Pixel Cat's End cats — even Generation 1 (starters, Nestor, business-card cats) — are not always homozygous. A cat showing a dominant trait may quietly carry a hidden recessive underneath. Unless a cat's full genetic string is revealed in-game, those hidden copies aren't visible.

How this tool handles it.

  • For a cat with no revealed string, it assumes the simplest genotype (homozygous) to turn the visible colour, pattern, wind, etc. into odds. Listing every hidden possibility would explode the number of strings and isn't practical.
  • In a kitten's genetic string, a ? is a single placeholder for an allele that could be a hidden recessive. We can't know which recessive is hiding, so we don't guess — one ?, nothing more.

Reading the placeholder:

  • [D?] — shows the dominant trait; the second copy is unknown (dominant, or a hidden recessive).
  • [rr] — shows the recessive trait, so both copies are known.
  • [??] — the gene is fully hidden (e.g. an accent colour the game never displays).

How a pair passes down (each parent gives one copy):

  • [rr] × [rr] → every kitten [rr] — fully known.
  • [D?] × [DD] → [DD] or [D?] — always shows dominant; may or may not carry the hidden copy.
  • [D?] × [D?] → [DD], [D?], or [??] — the last is fully unknown (it could even turn out [rr]), so it stays ??.
  • [??] × [??] → [??] — nothing to go on.

Once you can see a kitten's trait in its preview, the tool resolves it to [D?] or [rr]; only genuinely hidden genes stay [??]. Either way, outcomes are a close approximation — visible-trait percentages are exact given these assumptions, but hidden recessives mean a kitten can occasionally surprise you.
